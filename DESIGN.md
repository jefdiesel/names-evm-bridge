## 0. The ceiling, stated honestly

| Direction | What can be trustless on LEZ 0.4 | What blocks the rest |
|---|---|---|
| **Inbound, Ethereum→LEZ** | Yes, with the RFP-021 path: a sync-committee light client in a LEZ program, plus a storage proof of the vault's `isDepositRoot[root]` checked in the claimer's own execution. **Run (1):** Sepolia deposits claimed on the local LEZ chain through an attestation instance initialised at a real finalized checkpoint, public and private, with no stand-in attestor key (§3.3) | On the LEZ side, the sequencer enforces the claim's public part, as for every LEZ program |
| **Outbound, LEZ→Ethereum** | **No, for any design.** LEZ publishes no state root and never proves public execution (`lez/common/src/block.rs:51-67`, `lee/state_machine/src/state/mod.rs:158-175`, `lee/state_machine/src/program/mod.rs:121-135`). No Logos Blockchain light client works from headers alone, because PoL checks against ledger roots no header carries (`research/lez-and-l1-provability.md`) | Upstream would need (a) a ledger root in the Logos Blockchain header (a LIP) or (b) a LEZ zone validity prover |

So the design guarantees three things and is explicit about a fourth:

1. **The vault never pays out more than was deposited,** per token, even against a malicious sequencer committee (§5). This rests on Ethereum and proofs alone.
2. **The bridge adds no trust beyond LEZ's own.** The residual trust is that the staked committee does not publish invalid LEZ blocks.
3. **Fraud must be public before money moves.** It must be on the Logos Blockchain, under a slashable committee key, and inside a release delay that caps and freeze can act within. This needs G2 with Logos Blockchain finality, and the contest window. G2-lite (LEZ parsing and extraction, trusted tip) is **in progress**; the finality layer and the contest window are **not started (4)**.
4. **Closing the gap** (a) or (b) arrives as a **new vault version, not a redesign** (§10).

### 1.1 The core module in Basecamp: one call at a time, what its answers carry (checked 2026-11-14)

**Concurrency.** `lez_bridge` builds in the builder's `single` mode: `modules/lez_bridge/metadata.json`
says `"concurrency": "single"` (since 2026-11-14; before, it named none, and the pinned
`logos-module-builder` `de169fd` defaults to `single`, its `tests/test-parse-metadata.nix`). The
generated glue answers each call directly to its caller, on the module's one thread, and emits
no `__logos_call_complete__` events: those exist only in `multi` mode, where Basecamp hands every
answer, as an event, to every process holding a replica of the module (and a replica needs no
token). The module emits no events of its own either.

**What its answers carry.** No answer of `lez_bridge` carries a secret: the claim key `k` (and a
claim file's `masterSecret`), the exit secrets `k'`, the values `app_secret` gives, and the
half-signed claim the wallet returns stay in the module's memory (and in 0600 files a CLI call
reads and deletes); replies pass through `store::public_view` or `relayed::job_view`, and the
tests check that no key or signed transaction appears in a reply or in `wallet.json`. What the
answers do carry is public or the caller's own: deposit commitments and plans, job phases, request
ids, transaction hashes once sent, balances. The module is not a gatekeeper: any process can call it
without a token (a fact of Basecamp, not of this module), so it never answers with anything another
app must not see, and every action that spends or signs needs the person's approval in the Names app.

**Outside this module.** What the `names` module's own answers carry (it builds `multi`) is the
Names Wallet's to protect; this module's findings on it went to the Names Wallet's maintainers
(2026-11-14). One reply of this module carries something a person may treat as private: `config`
returns the Ethereum RPC URLs set in Settings, which can hold a provider's API key.

**A busy wallet (2026-11-14, Names `a342298`).** Any `names` call may answer
`{success: false, busy: true}` after waiting its turn for 15 s. That answer means the call was not
taken, so the module asks again after 2, 4, 6 and 8 s, starting no attempt more than 30 s after
the first (`wallet.rs` `names_try`), then says "Your Names Wallet is busy; nothing was sent; try
again." Polls (`request_status`, `resign_relayed_call` inside `relayed_claim_status`,
`wallet_request`) ask once and leave the retry to the next poll. Every wait is longer than the
15 s turn at the time (25 s for reads and proposals, 35 s for `app_secret`, 90 s for a relayed call's build);
since Names answers busy at once (af520ba, every 0.17.0 build) they are 8 s, 20 s and 45 s (branch `bridge-deadlock`). **No answer at all** (a timeout or a dropped
connection) means the call may have been taken. The wallet lets no app list its own pending
requests, so after an unanswered `propose_relayed_call` or `declare_programs` the module records it
(`wallet.json` `relay_uncertain`, for a request's 10-minute lifetime plus a minute) and answers
`{needs: "uncertain"}` instead of proposing again until the person has looked in the Names app and
pressed "Propose again" / "Continue" (`{"after_uncertain": true}`). A claim that already has a
running job gets that job back, so "Try again" after the screen stopped waiting never proposes a
second time. After an unanswered declaration, the next proposal shows whether the person approved
it (the wallet refuses calls to undeclared programs before anyone sees them), and the programs are
declared anew only if it did not. A claim job never waits for good on a silent wallet: past a
request's lifetime it ends ("nothing was signed or sent"), and Cancel while the wallet doesn't
answer stops following the request (it can't take it back; approved later, nothing posts it).
Private claims and burns (`propose_private_call`, which the wallet proves and sends itself): a
deposit whose claim is with the wallet gets that job back; an unanswered claim proposal is not
proposed again for 11 minutes (or until `after_uncertain`); an unanswered burn piece is never
proposed again from its plan, since an approved first one would burn twice (a new plan can).
The mini-app waits 120 s by default for the module (150 to 240 s for claims, recovery and
redemption) and polls one call at a time. (2): unit tests (`wallet.rs`, `tests_relayed.rs`,
`tests_names.rs`, `logic.test.mjs`); not yet run against a busy wallet in Basecamp.

**The freeze after a names reload (2026-11-14, branch `bridge-deadlock`).** A `lez_bridge` call
that calls names waits for the names replica in a nested Qt event loop on the module's one thread
(`QConnectedReplicaImplementation::waitForSource`). The generated dispatch took the module's
instance lock with a blocking `lock()`, so the next call delivered inside that loop blocked on the
lock the first call held, on the same thread: the loop never ran again, the first call's own
timeout could not fire, and the module never answered again (seen on two test machines
after names was reinstalled in a running Basecamp; reproduced with `modules/tests/repro-names-reload.sh
UNLOAD=1` in an isolated logosctl session). Fix: the scaffold's three lock sites use `try_lock`
(`rust-lib/build.rs`, `scaffold_patch.rs`, the names module's own fix of af520ba); a call that
arrives meanwhile answers `{success: false, busy: true}` at once, before any module code runs, and
the mini-app retries it. The build fails if the generator's lines change; the library carries
`lez-bridge-scaffold-try-lock=3`. The generated dispatch still holds the lock for the whole of a
call, so a call that needs names (`wallet_status`, `accounts`, the claim and deposit-request gates,
`wallet_request`, `relayed_claim_status` in its approval phase) holds it across that wait; the
polls that need no wallet (`config`, `registry`, `receives`, `receive_status`, `relayed_claim_check_with`
with `wallet_hint: false`) make no names call. (2): unit tests and the isolated repro.

## 2. Shared constants and formulas

The vault (`vault/src/BridgeVault.sol`) and the bridge (`lez-bridge/bridge/src/lib.rs`) use the same constants. `bridge/tests/flow.rs::hashes_and_tree_match_the_solidity_vault` checks them against vectors from `vault/test/Vectors.t.sol`.

- **`cfgId = SHA256(CFG_TAG ‖ u256(evmChainId) ‖ vault ‖ lezChainId ‖ zoneId ‖ programId)`**, with `CFG_TAG = keccak("RFP022/cfg/v1")`. It is 180 bytes of input, **derived on both sides, never supplied**:
  - The vault derives it from `block.chainid` and `address(this)`. The bridge derives it at `init` from its own program id.
  - Every e2e script stops unless the two agree. On Sepolia v2 the CLI `cfg-id`, the vault's `cfgId()` and the on-chain bridge config were all `0x7f1a…4b11` (`research/attested-claim-sepolia.md`).
  - **Since the 2026-11-14 security fixes (security-fixes branch; for the next vault, v3; the live v2 vault and demo keep the old programs):**
    - `lezChainId` = the LEZ chain's **Bedrock channel id**, declared at `Initialize` and never zero. LEZ rc3 gives a program no way to read its chain's identity (the channel id lives in the `sequencer_stake` config actor, written once at genesis by `InitChannelParams`, and that program answers no other program; the clock answers only block id and time). So the program cannot check it: it binds a vault to one *declared* chain. The CLI reads the chain's channel id from that config (`lez-bridge chain-id`), refuses an `init` that names another, and refuses a claim on a config that names another chain. In standalone mode (no Bedrock node, the demo chain) the channel id is a configured value, not a unique on-chain channel.
    - `zoneId` = the **root-source id** `SHA256("RFP022/source/v1" ‖ borsh(root source))`: `0x00` for the stand-in, `0x01 ‖ attestProgram ‖ instance ‖ u64le(slot)` for RFP-021, derived by the program, never supplied (`lez-bridge source-id`). The instance id is `SHA256(tag ‖ program ‖ borsh(InstanceConfig))`, so a vault built with it names on Ethereum the one checkpoint, fork schedule and genesis validators root its deposits are claimed through.
    - The vault's code and the formula are unchanged; only what the deployer passes as `lezChainId` and `zoneId` changes.
  - **Before (v1, v2, the live demo):** `lezChainId = zoneId = 0x00…00`, free `Init` inputs; the same Sepolia deposit was claimable once per chain instance (v2 leaf 3 was claimed on four, see the known issue below).
  - **Known issue (F15, S6): `cfgId` does not tell one LEZ chain from another.** A deposit is claimable once on every LEZ chain that runs the bridge at the same program id with a fresh nullifier set. It happened on the team's own chains: v2 leaf 3 was claimed on the local rc1 chain (block 2853), on the earlier rc1 demo chain, on the rc3 demo chain (block 30) and on a local rc3 chain (block 951, §9.0a). Nothing can be released from v2, so no value moved. Found by an independent review on 2026-11-14 (review A M1); fixed for the next vault and bridge on branch `security-fixes` as above (the channel id, declared and checked by the client; a program cannot read it on rc3). Trade-off: a re-genesis then strands every unclaimed deposit of that vault, which suits a real network and not a demo chain that resets.
- **Deposit commitment:** `C = SHA256("RFP022/dep" ‖ k ‖ dest ‖ token ‖ denom ‖ cfgId)`. The vault leaf is `SHA256(DEPOSIT_LEAF_TAG ‖ cfgId ‖ token ‖ denom ‖ C)`, with `DEPOSIT_LEAF_TAG = keccak("RFP022/deposit-leaf/v1")`.
- **Claim nullifier:** `nf_c = SHA256("RFP022/nfc" ‖ k ‖ dest ‖ token ‖ u256(denom))` (104 bytes in) since the 2026-11-14 fixes: it binds what the commitment binds, except `cfgId` (the nullifier PDA is the program's own). Before them (v1, v2, the live demo) it was `SHA256("RFP022/nfc" ‖ k)`, so anyone who learned `k` could make a cheaper deposit with the same `k` for themselves, claim it first and leave the victim's deposit unclaimable for good (review H2, `research/security-review-2026-11-14.md`). The vault never computes `nf_c` and is unaffected.
- **Exit commitment:** `E = SHA256("RFP022/exit" ‖ k_j ‖ ethDest ‖ token ‖ denom ‖ maxRelayFee ‖ cfgId)`. The exit leaf is `SHA256(EXIT_LEAF_TAG ‖ cfgId ‖ token ‖ denom ‖ E)`.
- **Release nullifier:** `nf_r = SHA256("RFP022/nfr" ‖ k_j)`.
- **Trees:** SHA-256, depth 32, with the Ethereum deposit contract's zero hashes.
- **Secrets:** derived from the wallet with `--key-index` (§10), or given raw with `--secret`; the CLI takes one or the other: a master secret `s` per position, `k = SHA256("RFP022/ck" ‖ s)` for deposits and `k' = SHA256("RFP022/ek" ‖ s')` for exits. A raw `--secret` has no `s`. The deposit page makes `s` at random in the browser and puts it in a claim file.

## 3. Inbound: deposit → claim (mint)

### 3.1 Deposit (Ethereum)

- `deposit(token, denomIndex, C)` and `depositETH(denomIndex, C)`.
  - The vault takes `denom + depositFee`, using SafeERC20 and an exact balance-delta check. Tokens that return no bool (USDT) work. Fee-on-transfer tokens are refused: `deposit` reverts with `WrongAmount` when the vault receives less than it pulled (`BridgeVault.sol` `deposit`). `release` has no such check; it pays with `safeTransfer`.
  - **Rebasing tokens are barred by registry policy, not by code.** `registerToken` checks only the decimals (`BadDecimals`) and the denomination list. A token whose balances change without transfers would break the vault's counters: a negative rebase leaves the last redeemers short, a positive one strands the surplus. So the deployer documentation (M7) lists rebasing and other balance-changing tokens (rebasing stablecoins, staking derivatives that rebase) as not registrable, and the admin must not register them; nothing in the vault detects them.
  - It appends the leaf, sets `isDepositRoot[root] = true` forever, and emits `Deposit(token, denomIndex, C, leafIndex, root)`. **No destination appears** (`test_DepositRevealsNoDestinationOrSecret`).
- **`commitmentUsed[C]`: a repeated commitment reverts** (`CommitmentUsed`). Run on anvil (run `public2`), and checked by `eth_call` on Sepolia v1 and v2 (`vault/DEPLOYMENTS.md`).
  - Reason: two deposits under the same `C` have the same `k`, `dest`, token and denomination, so the same `nf_c`: the second could never be claimed.
  - Front-running is **griefing only**. An attacker who copies a pending `C` makes the victim's deposit revert, but cannot claim their own copy without `k`, so they lose that deposit.
- **Delisting** (`deregisterToken`) stops new deposits only. Releases continue (`test_DeregisteredTokenStillReleases`).
- **Denominations (3), proposed defaults the admin can change:**
  - USDC/USDT/DAI: 100, 1,000 and 10,000;
  - ETH/WETH: 0.1, 1 and 10.
  - The Sepolia demo uses dUSDC 100/1,000/10,000 and ETH 0.01/0.1/1.
  - The mini-app's deposit plan splits a larger amount into denominations and spaces the pieces hours apart (`modules/lez_bridge/rust-lib/src/plan.rs`, unit-tested). The deposit page does not split.
  - **Anonymity set:** the earlier deposits of the same (token, denomination) under the root or anchor the claim asserts.

### 3.2 Claim (LEZ)

**Who mints.** Nothing mints native LEZ. Each wrapped definition's authority is the **bridge program id**:
- The runtime stamps each message's sender program. In private execution the circuit binds it to the proven image.
- `MintBy` mints only when that stamp equals the authority (`nobody_but_the_bridge_mints_the_wrapped_token`).
- **The authority is typed (since the 2026-11-14 fixes):** `Authority::Program(bridge)`. On LEZ rc3 a program id is its loader header's account id, an ordinary key-derived account whose key the deployer holds, and that key can sign transactions. Before the fix the token stored a bare account id, so the header key could sign `Mint` or `SetAuthority` as that id: unlimited unbacked mint and authority takeover (review C1; the old text here, "no key exists, and a program id cannot sign a rotation", was wrong). Now a program authority mints only through `MintBy` (the runtime's stamp of the sender's program, never a signature) and can never be rotated or renounced; an account authority never mints through `MintBy`. `lez-bridge register` reads the definition back and refuses one whose authority is not `Program(bridge)`. Tests: `c1_*` in `bridge/tests/review_2026_11_14.rs`, the `the_program_ids_own_key_*` token tests.
- Programs are deployed **immutable** (every run log prints "immutable").

**Initialisation (since the 2026-11-14 fixes).** `Initialize` / `InitializeAttested` must be signed by the program's own header account (the deployer's key; the instruction reaches that account's own actor of the bridge), carry the admin, freezer and attestor explicitly (none may be the header) and a non-zero `lezChainId`. Nobody can front-run it; after it the header key holds no power over the bridge. The old first-come `Init` / `InitAttested` (the first sender became admin and chose every role and the attestation source, review H1) are refused and stay in the enum only so older blocks decode.

**Claim, stand-in root source** (`Init` + `AttestRoot` + `Claim`), **(1)**, local runs and the public demo chain (operator-attested, `public-demo/attester/root-attester.py`):
1. An attestor key records vault roots (`AttestRoot`). This is the one trusted key.
2. In the claimer's actor:
   - recompute `C` and the leaf, and check the Merkle path to `root`;
   - send `AssertRoot` (the root PDA must exist) and `SpendClaim` (the nullifier PDA must be new);
   - send `Effect::Claim` to the config.
3. At the config:
   - refuse if minting is frozen, globally or for the token;
   - check the token and denomination, and add to `claimed_value`;
   - send `MintBy` for the **full denomination**.
   - There is no `registered` check, so a delisted token's earlier deposits stay claimable.

**Claim, RFP-021 root source** (`InitAttested` + `ClaimAttested`), **(1)**:
- **`InitAttested`** fixes `Config::root_source = Attest(AttestSource)`: attestation program, instance, chain id, vault, slot index. It checks that the instance exists. No instruction changes it.
- **In the claimer's actor (private for a private claimer):**
  - verify an `eth_getProof` account + storage proof that `isDepositRoot[root] == true`. The slot key is `keccak256(abi.encode(root, 83))`, `VAULT_DEPOSIT_ROOT_SLOT = 83`;
  - the proof is checked under an execution anchor (a state root, or a block hash plus header RLP; Sepolia under Gloas needs the latter);
  - proof nodes are validated before `alloy-trie` sees them, so a crafted proof is refused cleanly (`seeded_mutations_of_real_proofs_never_panic`, `structured_random_proofs_never_panic_and_the_node_check_is_sound`). A crafted proof used to panic inside `nybbles`;
  - plus the deposit's Merkle path.
- **Publicly:** only `AssertAnchor(instance, chain, anchor)` to the attestation program, plus the nullifier and the config effect.
  - The config refuses unless the attestation program, instance, slot index, vault and chain id are its own.
  - **The deposit root is not published.**
- **Slot 83:**
  - `isDepositRoot` 83, `g2State` 87, `_relayFeeMenu` 88, `relayFeeAllowed` 89 (verified 11-12 after the relay-fee work);
  - re-checked with `forge inspect … storage-layout` for the v2 deploy on 11-13 (`vault/DEPLOYMENTS.md`).
  - Command (a plain `forge inspect` fails with "storage layout missing from artifact"):
    ```sh
    FOUNDRY_EXTRA_OUTPUT='["storageLayout"]' forge inspect --force BridgeVault storage-layout
    ```
- The two sources refuse each other's root recording and claims (`the_sources_do_not_mix`, `an_attested_config_refuses_the_stand_in_root_under_any_flag`).

### 3.4 Refusals (Privacy #5, Usability #8)

From the crate docs of `bridge/src/lib.rs` ("Refusals"), checked on chain with v2 (`research/attested-claim-sepolia.md`):

- **One text for every deposit-dependent claim failure: `claim refused`.** That covers:
  - a witness that proves no deposit under its root (wrong leaf for this secret, signer, token, amount or cfg id; a malformed path or index; a storage proof or anchor witness that doesn't verify);
  - a root no attestor recorded;
  - a spent nullifier (a repeat claim).
- **No panic text in the claim, burn or root paths carries a value** (`no_program_panic_can_print_a_value`, `every_attested_claim_refusal_is_the_same_for_two_deposits`).
- **Texts that depend only on public data** stay distinct, so Usability #8 can name them:
  - `minting is frozen`;
  - `claim for another deployment`;
  - `claim for another attestation source`;
  - `token not registered`;
  - `bad denomination`;
  - `wrong amount for this denomination`;
  - the source mismatch;
  - `anchor not finalized by this instance` / `anchor is for another chain`.
  - Burn refusals speak only of the definition and amount, which the burn publishes anyway.
- **On chain (v2, dry runs with `--no-precheck`):**
  - the four deposit-dependent cases answer exactly `claim refused`: tampered storage node, value 0, another block's header under anchor A, and a repeat claim;
  - an anchor A never recorded, or instance B's anchor, fails earlier in the LEZ runtime as "Program violated execution rules". No anchor PDA exists under A, and the bridge cannot seed another program's PDA.
  - That error differs from `claim refused`, but it depends only on public data (which anchors A recorded), not on the deposit.
- **The CLI diagnoses locally before sending** (root not attested, anchor not finalized, proof does not verify, already claimed, frozen, wrong denomination), by running the same checks natively. Those messages carry trie paths and node bytes, and they never leave the machine.
- **What the program cannot control:**
  - **A claim that passes simulation and then loses a race** (a freeze, the same claim sent twice) is included and reverted, charged its full declared gas. Which step failed is inferable from public state. A repeat names the same nullifier PDA as the claim that spent it, which links the two transactions, not the deposit.
    - Run: the v2 repeat claim sent with the pre-check bypassed was included and reverted in block 2855, charged 16,058,248 (2,000,000 declared gas); counters and supply unchanged.
  - **A public-account claim publishes its secret `k` by design**, so it links the Sepolia depositor to the claimer (block 2209, 2853).
  - **There is no claim cap on LEZ**, so "cap rejection" (PP5) has no LEZ counterpart. A cap added later must use the same text, after the nullifier.

### 3.5 One-time recipient and the fee float, (1)

- **One-time address.** `Claim`/`ClaimAttested` carry `recipient_opening: Option<[u8;32]>`.
  - With `Some(o)`, the claimer's actor mints to `A = V.blinded(o)`, which is `SHA256(tag ‖ V ‖ o)` (`lee/state_machine/core/src/program/mod.rs:248-264`), computed from its own resolved id `V`.
  - `C` still binds `V`, so another account with the same `k` and `o` is refused (`an_opening_does_not_let_another_account_claim`).
  - The wallet delivers the credit to `V` inside the proof, because the opening is in `V`'s witness (`lez/wallet/src/lib.rs:1590-1604`).
  - **Default for a private claimer:** `o = SHA256("RFP022/alias" ‖ k)`. `--reveal-recipient` opts out (mint to `V` by name, no sponsorship).
  - A public claimer always mints to itself.
- **Float.**
  - The config account holds native LEZ that anyone tops up (`fund-float`).
  - `sponsor_amount` (admin `SetSponsor`; 0 = off; at most `MAX_SPONSOR_AMOUNT` = 50,000,000 since the 2026-11-14 fixes, review M2) is paid to `A` from the float in the claim's own transaction (`native_token::custody_transfer` under the config seed). `V` pays its LEZ fee from that payment.
  - At most once per deposit (the nullifier).
  - The float has no withdrawal path.
- **Limits:**
  - **No per-claim opt-out** while `sponsor_amount > 0`.
  - **An empty float blocks one-time claims** until topped up (`an_empty_float_fails_the_claim_cleanly`).
  - **Burns are not sponsored:** the burner's sender is a wallet-chosen presentation alias, not a witnessed opening.
- **PDA effects carry seeds** (`with_pda_seeds`), so LEZ admits new PDAs by seed (`every_effect_carries_the_seed_of_its_pda`).
- **Relayer for claims (3):**
  - R co-signs and pays A a native transfer; the config would mint a fixed `relay_fee` to R.
  - Not chosen, because the claimer would hold `denom − relay_fee`, which can't exit as one denomination.
  - Unverified: whether the stock wallet can prove with a co-signer whose key it doesn't hold.
- **Public claims are not sponsored, and on LEZ rc3 cannot be (checked in the rc3 source, 11-13):** a public transaction's fee payer must sign it (`lee::is_fee_authorized`: the payer's signature must be in the witness set), so a program-owned account (the float) cannot be the payer; and settlement holds the fee reserve from the payer *before* executing (`lez/chain_state/src/apply.rs`, "Phase 1: Reserve"), so a credit from the float inside the claim arrives too late. A zero-balance public claim is refused at admission and charges nothing (rc3 test chain: "holds 0 but the fee reserve is 1536824"). Whoever pays a public claim that reverts pays its whole declared gas: 16,015,144 observed at the default 2M gas (rc3 test chain, block 38); at the wallet's measured limit (190,210) it would be about 1.5M (computed, not observed). Zero-balance public claimers: the relayed claim, a co-signing fee payer (§3.6).
- **In the mini-app (2):** a privacy indicator for every flow (`privacy.rs`), the private account pre-selected, a suggested claim time, and every weaker choice listed with what it leaks. Unit-tested and rendered, not run by a user. A randomised claim delay enforced by a client: (4).

### 3.6 Demo-network bridge fee: the deposit desk and the relayed claim

A **demo-network convenience, not part of the trust-minimised bridge.** It lets a claimer with an empty LEZ account make a public claim on the bridge test network without anyone acting by hand: the depositor pays a fee on Ethereum, the operator pays the Logos side by co-signing the claim as its fee payer.

- **What it is (Ethereum side), (1) Sepolia.** `vault/src/desk/DepositDesk.sol`, a contract in front of the immutable Sepolia v2 vault, deployed at `0x77E37E1D6e886d8fb988496B9c2EfF3E78e27DF2` (`vault/DEPLOYMENTS.md`). One transaction for the depositor: `depositETHWithFee(denomIndex, C, lezPublicKey)` or `depositWithFee(token, denomIndex, C, lezPublicKey)`. The desk takes the vault's amount (denomination + the vault's own deposit fee, 0 on v2) plus a **bridge fee of `feeBps` of the denomination in the same asset**: an owner-set rate, capped at 300 bps (3%) by a constant; 300 at deploy, **30 bps (0.3%) since 2026-11-14** (`setFeeBps(30)`, tx `0xdf39c20d…05cd`, Sepolia block 12132632, sent with `vault/script/SepoliaDeskSetFee.s.sol`; `feeBps()` reads 30 through PublicNode); rounded down; every current demo denomination divides exactly at both rates. The deposit page reads the rate on chain, forwards the vault's amount to `depositETH`/`deposit` in the same call, keeps the fee and emits `FeePaid(token, lezPublicKey, C, denomIndex, leafIndex, fee)` for **every** deposit. ETH: `msg.value` must equal the total exactly. ERC-20: one `transferFrom` of the total (the page approves the desk for exactly that), then an exact approval of the vault, which pulls its amount. **The page sends `lezPublicKey` = 0 on every deposit** (the field is a leftover of the fee-grant design below; the contract is immutable).
- **Same leaf.** The vault binds only token, denomination and `C` (`depositLeaf`), never the caller: a deposit through the desk is the same leaf in the same tree as a direct one (`test_EthLeafIdenticalToDirectDeposit`, `test_Erc20LeafIdenticalToDirectDepositWithVaultFee`, against the real `BridgeVault`).
- **What it can do:** hold collected bridge fees until the owner withdraws them (`withdrawFees(token, to)`, only the recorded fees, never stray tokens); let the owner set `feeBps` anywhere in 0..300 (`MAX_FEE_BPS`, a constant).
- **What it can't do:** keep, redirect or delay a deposit (the vault records it in the same call or everything reverts; `testFuzz_DeskHoldsOnlyFees`); charge more than 3%; charge a fee other than the quoted one (exact amounts: a fee change between the page's quote and the transaction reverts it, `test_FeeChangeAfterQuoteReverts`); touch the vault (no role there); be upgraded; receive ETH outside a deposit. Reentrancy-guarded; a fee-on-transfer token is refused.
- **Why an account id cannot simply be funded (LEZ rc3).** Every delivery to a public actor passes `Executor::admit` (`lee/state_machine/core/src/execution_state.rs`, `admit`): it is admitted only if the account is already in state, or is a signer or in the transaction's `admission_evidence` (a public key, or a PDA's program and seed; `lee/state_machine/src/validated_state_diff/public_backend.rs`, `admits`), or is a PDA the sending program seeds. Otherwise `UnadmittedPublicActor`, which the chain reports as "Program violated execution rules". LEZ's own test `a_call_to_an_absent_public_account_needs_evidence_deriving_it` (`lee/state_machine/src/state/tests/pending_messages.rs`) pins it. An account id is `SHA256(prefix ‖ pk)`, so the id alone gives no evidence: a plain transfer to a never-used id is included, reverts and charges the sender its fee; a program (the bridge) crediting an arbitrary id hits the same check; genesis and Bedrock deposits take a key or PDA too (`lez/programs/bridge/core/src/deposit.rs`). An account already in state is credited by id. (Checked on a throwaway chain of the retired rc1 on 11-13: the plain transfer to a never-used id reverted; to an existing id it was credited. rc1 is retired: everything else here is rc3.)
- **The relayed claim (LEZ side).** The fee payer of a public transaction is named in its `FeeDeclaration` and must sign it (`lee::is_fee_authorized`; `lez/chain_state/src/apply.rs` "Phase 1: Reserve" holds the reserve from the payer before the claim runs), and the signers must be exactly the message's nonce holders and `authorized_accounts` (`check_signers`). Nothing requires the payer to be the claimer: the stock rc3 wallet itself co-signs as an extra payer that is not one of the transaction's accounts (`send_public`, "co-signs (nonce and signature appended)"). So a public claim can be signed by the claimer, whose signature admits its never-used account, and paid by a second signer, the relay's payer:
  - `lez-bridge claim --relay` (`lez-bridge/cli/src/relay.rs`): plans the claim and runs the CLI's pre-check as today, measures it with the wallet's own dry run (gas, `max_fee`, revert check), then builds the same message with the payer added (nonce, authorized account, `FeeDeclaration.payer`, `max_fee` scaled for the larger transaction), signs it with the claimer's key and sends it to the relay through the chain's own endpoint (JSON-RPC `bridge_relayInfo` and `bridge_relayClaim`, which the demo chain's RPC filter, `public-demo/proxy/lez-rpc-proxy.py --relay`, answers from the relay on loopback with its own per-client bucket and body cap; no extra public path); the relay co-signs and submits; the CLI confirms inclusion as for any claim. Up to three tries when the payer's nonce moved; `--dry-run` asks the relay to check only. The claimer needs no native LEZ. The direct, self-paid claim is unchanged.
  - `public-demo/relayer/relayer.py` with `lez-bridge relay-cosign` (operator side): strict decode (exactly one signature, the claimer's; signers exactly {claimer, payer}; no admission evidence, no promotions, no tip; gas ≤ `MAX_GAS`, `max_fee` ≤ `MAX_FEE`; the root call is the claimer's actor of this bridge; the instruction is a public stand-in `Claim`; the declared actors are exactly a public claim's six; none is the payer's), current nonces, the CLI's claim pre-checks (attested root, unspent nullifier, minting open, denomination, the deposit in the root), a simulation that must apply, then the payer's signature and the submission. Once per deposit (claim nullifier; `MAX_RELAYS=2` allows one retry when the first did not apply, its nullifier still unspent 90 s later); caps per IP and per UTC day; a floor on the payer's balance; optionally (`DESK_STATE`) only deposits made through the desk; every decision logged; the intent is on disk before the payer signs. The payer is a **dedicated account** (it refuses a payer that holds a bridge role), because a signer is authorized in the execution.
  - **The payer's signature binds the whole message** (`Message::hash`, which covers the nonces, the actors, the instruction and the fee): it is valid for that one transaction only; a replay of the included transaction is refused (stale nonce).
  - **What a griefer can cost:** the relay pays only for claims of real deposits under an attested root, once each. Known ways a checked claim can still revert: the bridge admin or freezer freezing minting before it lands. On the live v2 programs a second deposit with the same secret to another account could also revert it (review B L1, run by the reviewer: this text used to say only the admin or freezer could); with the fixed nullifier (bound to the claimer, token and denomination) only the claimer can spend its nullifier, and any transaction the claimer signs moves its nonce, so a relayed claim queued behind it is refused free of charge (a nonce mismatch is `ExecutionCharge::FREE`, `lee/state_machine/src/validated_state_diff/mod.rs`); a reverted co-signed claim charges the payer at most its `max_fee`. ## 4. Outbound: burn → G2 → release

1. **Burn (LEZ), (1), local.**
   - The token's `BurnWithNotice` destroys exactly one denomination and tells the config `(token, denom, E)`. The config:
     - checks the burn freeze (global and per token);
     - checks that the amount is a denomination;
     - adds to `burned_value` and asserts `burned_value ≤ claimed_value`;
     - emits `Exited { token, denom, E }`.
   - Private burns put `E` in the transaction (run `private5` block 884). Public burns reveal the burning account but not `ethDest` (`a_burn_publishes_exactly_the_allowlist`).
2. **G2 state update: Logos Blockchain finality (4); G2-lite in progress.**
   - **Designed:** a permissionless prover proves the burns and claims finalised since the last update. The vault's `update(journal, seal)` is built and tested with a mock verifier (2). It checks:
     - the seal against the immutable `updateImageId`;
     - `cfgId`, and `prevState == g2State`;
     - `newState != prevState` (`NoStateAdvance`, commit `146379d`): a non-advancing journal would stay valid after it is applied, so anyone could replay it and re-add its claims and exits. **Open:** a journal that returns to an earlier state (A → B → A) is not refused on chain; only G2's forward-moving LEZ tip prevents it;
     - every `depositRootsUsed` entry is in `isDepositRoot`;
     - per-(token, denom) `claims ≤ deposits`;
     - per-token `burnedValue ≤ claimedValue`.
   - It then appends the exit leaves and records `exitRootTime[root]` (batch-end roots only).
   - **G2 has three parts:**
     - (i) Logos Blockchain finality from a checkpoint: PoL Groth16 verify, Ed25519, Blake2b, epoch nonce, k-deep. Ledger roots come from a header commitment (LIP) or a Mantle replay, behind one `LedgerRootSource` interface;
     - (ii) the LEZ channel's inscriptions, the `MsgId` and `prev_block_hash` chains, and borsh parsing;
     - (iii) burn extraction (private: compose the succinct receipt against the pinned `PRIVACY_PRESERVING_CIRCUIT_ID`; public: read `E` from the instruction) and claim extraction (receipt verified, deposit root listed, `nf_c` added to a sorted accumulator carried in `g2State`).
   - **G2-lite, in progress** (`lez-bridge/outbound/`; crate docs of `outbound/core/src/lib.rs`):
     - parts (ii) and (iii), with the LEZ tip trusted (`TrustedTip`, test builds only); the release build's finality source is a placeholder that refuses every batch;
     - **Trust under the trusted tip (since 2026-11-15, branch `outbound-prove-31`):** the trusted-tip guest (`outbound_guest_trusted_tip`, feature `unchecked-private`) no longer checks privacy-circuit receipts. Private claims and burns stand on the sequencer's word, as public ones already did: G2-lite checks no public signatures and takes public execution as the sequencer applied it, and under `TrustedTip` the blocks themselves are final only because the operator says so. What G2 still proves under it: block linkage and hashes, that every claim, burn, root and registry change it counts is in those blocks and is one the bridge program would accept, the nullifier accumulator (no double claim), and the journal. Why: RISC Zero advisory GHSA-g6qg-9j8p-7j87 (risc0 < 3.1.0 accepts forged claims); the provers moved to risc0-zkvm 3.1.0, whose verifier refuses every 3.0.x receipt, and LEZ v0.4.0-rc3 makes its privacy receipts with 3.0.5, so a checking guest could never prove past one private claim or burn, and updates chain. **What the light-client stage restores:** finality from the Logos Blockchain (part (i)) instead of the operator, and, once LEZ makes its privacy receipts with a patched risc0, the receipt check (`env::verify` against the pinned circuit, kept in the light-client build `outbound_guest`); public execution stays on the sequencer committee until LEZ proves it (`outbound/core/src/lib.rs`, "Trust");
     - the claim accumulator is an indexed Merkle tree keyed by the claim-nullifier PDA (`outbound/claim-acc`);
     - `vault/test/OutboundVector.t.sol` feeds its journals, made from the real LEZ blocks of run `public9`, to `update` (2 tests, in CI);
     - local round trips (`scripts/e2e-roundtrip.sh`: deposit, claim, burn, G2-lite update, release; public accounts; mock-verified, LEZ finality trusted) ran on 11-13. Run `roundtrip1` reached its end after one failed setup check (`vault g2State is not the G2 genesis state`) and a resume; run `roundtrip2` ran unattended to its end with no failed step: updates, 7 releases, the solvency checks, and a search of the release calldata for 14 linking values (0 found). Its `ReleaseTooEarly` case was not exercised. The worker's report was reviewed and the logs spot-checked, not re-run.
     - over the public demo chain (2026-11-15, executor only): blocks 4486..9629 with the v3 bridge, 106.0M user cycles, 127 segments (po2 20), about 20k cycles a LEZ block whatever its content, about 113M a day of chain; the journal carries the burn of block 9615, but the v3 vault cannot take it (`g2State` zero, `vault/DEPLOYMENTS.md`);

     1. **payees:** recipient = the vault or `address(0)` → `BadRecipient` (`test_ReleaseToVaultOrZeroRefused`);
     2. freeze;
     3. denomination;
     4. known exit root and its delay;
     5. nullifier;
     6. relay fee: a non-zero fee needs a relayer (`ZeroRelayer`), a relayer that is not the vault (`BadRelayer`), and a value on the admin menu (`RelayFeeNotOnMenu`);

   - **Delay rule** (`releasableAt(token, root)`, `releaseDelayState(token)`, `delaySteps(token, i)`):
     - an exit root recorded at time t waits the **lowest per-token delay in force at any moment since t**;
     - a raise applies only to roots recorded after it, never lengthens a recorded root's wait, and never makes a releasable root wait again;
     - the history is searched by binary search (`test_GasReleaseVsDelayHistory`);
     - tests: `testFuzz_DelayChangesNeverReblock`, `test_LaterLoweringNeverLengthensRecordedWait`.
     - **Edge:** a root recorded in the same second as a raise gets the lower delay. The release freeze is the emergency tool.
   - **User errors the vault cannot detect:**
     - an ETH recipient contract without a receive function (`test_RevertEthPayoutRejected`);
     - an issuer-blacklisted recipient.
     - In both cases the exit is stuck, because the recipient is fixed in `E`, but nothing is stranded for others. Clients must warn before building `E`.

## 5. Conservation bound, as implemented

The vault enforces this per token (`BridgeVault.sol` header comment, `update`, `release`):

`released value ≤ burned value ≤ claimed value ≤ deposited value`

- **Claims ≤ deposits,** per (token, denom); the G2 journal may only cite deposit roots the vault recorded.
- **Burns ≤ claims, by value per token,** so fragments are redeemable: ten 100s exit against one claimed 1000 (`test_BurnInOtherDenominationByValue`, `fragments_redeem_by_value`; run in `public2`).
- **Releases ≤ burns** per (token, denom).
- **The LEZ side mirrors burns ≤ claims** (`bridge/src/lib.rs`).
- **Tests:**
  - `testFuzz_Solvency`;
  - the stateful invariant suite `Invariant.t.sol` (`invariant_all`, with handlers for the vault and the admin; `test_SeededDriverCoverage`);
  - the cross-side property `value_is_conserved_across_random_interleavings_and_the_trace_fixture_is_current` (`bridge/tests/conservation.rs` with `fixtures/conservation_trace.json`).
- **What a fully malicious committee can still do (once G2 exists):** forge burns up to the claimed-but-unburned value, taking from LEZ holders. **It can never take more than was deposited**, and only within release caps and the delay.

## 7. Trust model

| Party | Can affect | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Ethereum (finality, sync committee ⅔ for the RFP-021 path) | Inbound safety. A sync committee with ≥ 342 of 512 seats colluding can sign a false header, unslashable; once G2 exists, forged claims can then take unclaimed deposits within caps, delay and freeze; cost and bounds in §9.8 | Take more than the deposited-but-unclaimed value per (token, denomination) (§9.8) |
| RISC Zero proof systems | The STARK (zkVM, LEZ privacy circuit): no trusted setup. **The Groth16 wrap the vault verifies (G2 `update`, G3 `release`) rests on a trusted setup:** phase 1 the Hermez powers of tau (2^23), phase 2 RISC Zero's own ceremony run with p0tion and coordinated with PSE (risc0 docs, `website/api_versioned_docs/version-3.0/trusted-setup-ceremony.md`, read 2026-11-14). It is sound if at least one contributor in each phase destroyed their secret. Whoever held the whole secret could forge seals: G2 updates and G3 releases, bounded by the vault's conservation rule, caps, delay and freeze (§5) | Touch the inbound path (no Groth16 on LEZ) |
| LEZ sequencer committee | Liveness. **Outbound safety for burns up to claimed value** (the §0 gap) | Release more than was deposited (§5) |
| Stand-in attestor (local stand-in runs and the public demo chain's operator attester) | Inbound safety: it can attest a fake root, so it can mint unbacked wrapped tokens on that chain | Exists only with `root_source = stand-in`. The attested Sepolia runs on the local chain have none; the public demo chain has one (operator-attested) and is bound to a vault that can never release |
| Attestation submitters (updaters) | Liveness only: an update is accepted on proof validity alone | Insert a header the committee did not sign |
| G2 provers / updaters | Liveness only (once built); fork view bounded by the contest window | Forge a G2 proof |
| Release relayers (Ethereum) | Liveness only: recipient, relayer and fee are bound in the G3 journal | Redirect or overcharge (`test_RevertRelayerRedirectsOrOvercharges`) |
| Claim relay (LEZ fee payer; one operator today, §3.6) | Liveness only: it can withhold its co-signature (the claimer then pays its own fee). It sees the claim, including a public claim's secret `k`, before inclusion, and the claimer's IP address | Change the claim (the claimer's signature covers the message) or take the mint. Open, paid relays: `research/open-settlement.md` |
| Admin | Registry, caps, fees ≤ 2%, relay-fee menu, delay ≥ immutable minimum, sponsor amount, unfreeze | Change the verifier or image ids (immutable), touch principal |
| Freezer | Freeze | Unfreeze, move funds |
| Deployer (holder of the bridge's loader header key; on LEZ rc3 the program id is that account's id) | **Fixed programs:** one `Initialize` (picks admin, freezer, attestor, chain id and, through the root source, the attestation instance), then nothing; it is also the only one who can re-deploy the same program id on another chain (the M1 residual, `research/security-review-2026-11-14.md`). **Live v2 programs (before the fixes):** unlimited mint of every wrapped token and mint-authority takeover (review C1), and an unsigned-race `Init` at every re-genesis (H1) | Fixed programs: mint, rotate the authority, take a role, re-initialise |
| Token issuer | Blacklist the vault or a recipient, upgrade the token | — (inherited, per the spec) |

**The freeze works only at token and protocol level.** It can't target one user's holdings, because the protocol does not know whose are whose. This is by design (spec, Trust model).

**Updater economics:**
- A finality update proved in **7,074.7 s** at 9,483 MB (rc1, §9.0) and in **7,283.0 s** at 9,457 MB (rc3, §9.0a) on an Apple M4 (16 GB).
- So a desktop CPU makes at most one update every ~2 h, and a faster submitter invalidates the work (the store refuses a stale `prev`).
- Practical updaters need faster hardware or GPU proving, as long as updates are proved privately.
- A public update removes the proof: a submitter then pays only gas. For an in-period finality update it is built and ran on a local rc3 chain (one public transaction, 9.29M cycles, §9.3b, branch `public-update`); period updates and low participation still need M3's two steps (§9.3a) or the proof.
- Nobody is paid for an update today. A reward paid per accepted update, without making the role exclusive (RFP-021 Soft #5), is designed in `research/open-settlement.md` (3), not built.

### 8.3 How LEZ 0.4 moves value to private accounts and pays fees

This summarises an 11-12 privacy design note. Only the references marked ✓ were re-opened; the rest are cited as the note gave them. Lines as of rc1.

**Three ways a private account receives value:**
1. **Same transaction, credited inside the proof.** A public actor's sync message, or an async one promoted through `public_context.promotions`, becomes a `PublicToPrivate` boundary step. Its `envelope.to` and message are journaled (`execution_state.rs:131-137,919-952,1372-1399`; `circuit_io.rs:498-515` ✓). **Our claim uses this.** It publishes the recipient address: `V` before 11-12, the one-time address `A` now.
2. **Unpromoted public→private async.** Published as `Publication::Clear{body, recovery}`, with `to` and the amount in clear (`validated_state_diff/mod.rs:522-532,572-575`; `circuit_io.rs:222-249`).
3. **Private→private async.** Sealed: only a commitment and an encrypted note (`lee/state_machine/core/src/sealing.rs:30-66`). A private→private token `Transfer` hides from, to and amount (`lez-bridge/token/src/lib.rs:174-185,317-320`).

**Aliases.**
- Any `to` may be a **blinded alias** `id.blinded(r)` (`program/mod.rs:248-264`), resolved only if the witness carries `r` (`circuit_io.rs:276-278`; `execution_state.rs:997-1001,1235-1262`).
- The wallet auto-promotes messages addressed to a witnessed alias (`lez/wallet/src/lib.rs:1590-1604` ✓).
- Senders are masked as `SenderPresentation::Blinded` (`execution_state.rs:1409-1429`).
- The fee payer gets an alias with a recovery binding (`lez/wallet/src/account_manager.rs:730-749,789-828`).

**Fees for a private transaction:**
- **Exactly one fee credit:** one native `Credit` crossing `PrivateToPublic` into the fee inbox (`lez/chain_state/src/classify.rs:65-93,117-143`), from a witnessed private account holding its own key (`execution_state.rs:1068-1080,1120-1144`).
- **Surplus** is refunded to `refund_to` or published to the alias (`lez/chain_state/src/apply.rs:510-543`).
- **The fee may come from value credited earlier in the same transaction.** The float uses this.
- **Not offered by 0.4:** a sponsor field for private transactions, a fee in the wrapped token, a fee drawn from the mint.

### 8.4 What LEZ 0.4 cannot hide

- **No address-free credit.** A public program cannot credit a private account without publishing some address (an alias at best): `execution_state.rs:131-137,919-952`; `circuit_io.rs:498-515` ✓.
- **No third-party payer for a private fee.** Public transactions accept only a co-signing payer: `lee/state_machine/src/fees.rs:121-138` ✓.
- **No split proofs.** One transaction's proof cannot be split between parties: `circuit_io.rs:182-203`; `lee/privacy_preserving_circuit/src/main.rs:16-136`.
- **Minted amounts are public.** The `Credit` amount and supply: `lez-bridge/token/src/lib.rs:370-387`.
- **No on-chain randomness.** Openings must come from the witness (inferred, `program/mod.rs:372-438`).
- **No events from private execution.** `EMITS_EVENTS = false` (`research/lez-and-l1-provability.md`).

### 8.5 Not met yet

- **PP2 / PP3 outbound:** these need G2 and a real release.
- **PP4:** `k`, `o` and `V` stay on the user's machine today. A remote Groth16 wrap service (§4, step 4) would be a new component handling user data.
- **PP6:** what a relayer learns is designed (§3.5) but untested.
- **PP5:** done for the deposit-dependent claim failures (§3.4), with the stated limits: no LEZ cap, late failures, and the runtime-level anchor error.

### 9.1 Accounts

All accounts are PDAs of `eth_attest` (`attest/core/src/lib.rs` docs):

### 9.2 Update placement and the trust argument

From `attest/program/src/lib.rs` docs:

- **Why today's update can't be public.** One update with today's verifier costs about 37M user cycles, but LEZ caps execution gas at 10M per transaction **and** per block:
  - `lez/programs/fee/core/src/market.rs`: `MAX_GAS_EXEC = 2 * TARGET_GAS_EXEC = 10,000,000` ✓;
  - `validity.rs` `GasLimitAboveCap`; `accumulate_exec_gas`.
- **Where it runs instead.** `Update` is verified in the **submitter's own actor** as a private, locally proved execution, which is not metered (`lee/state_machine/src/privacy_preserving_transaction/circuit/mod.rs:237-242` ✓). Only a ~520-byte `Effect::Advance { prev, next }` reaches the public store (519 bytes in `out-update.log`).
- **Why the store can trust `Advance`:**
  1. **The sender is this program's code.** The runtime stamps the sender program even for a blinded private sender (`execution_state.rs:1407-1428`).
  2. **The code is really this image.** A disclosed image claim must equal the live image (`validated_state_diff/mod.rs:624-647`); an undisclosed one must be an **immutable** program header (`circuit_io.rs:71-94`). Hence the immutable deploy (VS3).
  3. **`Advance` is built only right after `verify_update` succeeds** for exactly that `(config, prev)`.
  4. **The store compares `prev` with its own state.** A second racing submitter fails cleanly (R8).
  5. **The store checks progress:** a later finalized slot or a newly learned committee.
- **Submitter identity plays no part** (F11, VS6). The proved update came from a fresh private account with no prior relationship to the instance.
- **`Init` follows the same pattern** in the initialiser's actor. It fits the public cap (9.1M), so it ran public.
- **Consumers** verify inclusion (storage slot, account field, receipt log; `Eq`/`Ne` predicates; deterministic statement ids) in their own actor, then send `AssertAnchor(instance, chain, anchor)`, the only public trace (F7).

### 9.6 A stalled instance, and how it catches up (RFP-021 R7, F10)

Read on 2026-11-14 in `attest/program/src/lc.rs` (`verify_update`) and Helios consensus-core at rev `2af9b418` (`ethereum/consensus-core/src/consensus_core.rs`: `verify_generic_update`, `apply_generic_update`, `apply_update_no_quorum_check`). An earlier version of this proposal said an instance more than one sync-committee period behind "cannot advance again". That was wrong; this section replaces it.

**The rule.** Helios, like the spec's `validate_light_client_update`, accepts an update only if its signature slot is in the store's sync-committee period, or in the next one when the store knows the next committee. The store's period is the period of its **finalized header**, not the wall clock. `verify_update` passes the update's own signature slot as Helios's `expected_current_slot` and adds only a lower time bound (`not_before_ms`, the start of the signature slot, enforced by the sequencer through the response's timestamp window). There is no upper bound, so an old update is as acceptable as a fresh one.

**Catch-up.** A period update (an item of `/eth/v1/beacon/light_client/updates`) carries the next committee. Applied to a store that knows its next committee, an update whose finalized header lies in the next period rotates the committees (current ← next, next ← the update's) and moves the finalized header into that period. Applied to a store that does not know its next committee, an update signed and finalized in the store's period records it. So an instance whose store sits in period P while Ethereum is in period N catches up by submitting, in order:
1. the period-P update, if the store does not know its next committee (after a rotation by a finality update it does not: `committee_handoff_…` checks `next_committee == None`);
2. the update of each period P+1 … N−1, each rotating one step;
3. a current finality update, signed in period N.

The anchors recorded before the stall stay valid throughout (R3; anchor PDAs are never deleted). No manual repair is needed, and nothing is skipped: each step verifies the handoff before it.

**What each step needs.**
- The update's finalized header must lie in the same period as its attested header, or the store does not rotate and the next period's update is then refused ("invalid sync committee period"). The spec's `is_better_update` ranks such updates first, and full nodes SHOULD serve the best update of each period (`specs/altair/light-client/full-node.md`), so the served update normally qualifies. A period with no qualifying update would block the catch-up; we have not seen one and have no test for it.
- At least `min_participants` signers (≥ 342 of 512).
- Someone must still serve it. Full nodes SHOULD serve each period's best update for the epochs `[current − compute_min_epochs_for_block_requests(), current]` (`full-node.md`). With mainnet constants that is 256 + 65,536 / 2 = **33,024 epochs, about 146.8 days**, by the phase-0 formula (`specs/phase0/p2p-interface.md`), and **14,299 epochs, about 63.6 days**, by the Gloas formula (`specs/gloas/p2p-interface.md`; `CHURN_LIMIT_QUOTIENT_GLOAS` 32,768 and `CONSOLIDATION_CHURN_LIMIT_QUOTIENT` 65,536 from `configs/mainnet.yaml`). Computed by us from consensus-specs at `313cbfe` (2026-11-13). It is a SHOULD, so a provider may keep less.
- **The weak-subjectivity period bounds a safe catch-up.** Beyond it, members of old committees may have exited and could sign another chain at no cost; RFP-021 F10 then asks for a new instance. Electra's guide (the rule mainnet runs today) gives **3,532 epochs, about 15.7 days**, for a total active balance of 8.4M ETH or more at the default safety decay of 10 (`specs/electra/weak-subjectivity.md`, reference table). Gloas's guide (the rule Sepolia runs) gives **1,348 to 1,611 epochs, about 6 to 7 days**, for 4.2M to 67M ETH (`specs/gloas/weak-subjectivity.md`, reference table). Which value applies depends on the chain's fork and active balance. The instance does not enforce this yet (weak-subjectivity rule, M3): today an operator could catch an instance up past that bound.

**Cost.** One private proof per missed period. A period update is 46.3M executor cycles (§9.3) and has never been proved. Scaled from the rc3 finality update (37.2M cycles, 7,283.0 s), it would take about 2.5 h on our desktop CPU: **an estimate**. So a stall of n periods costs about n × 2.5 h of proving before a new anchor appears, at today's cycle counts. With M3's public path (§9.3a, not built) each missed period would instead be three public transactions (about 4M, 3.3M and 4M cycles, estimated, with the second Helios patch), paid in gas.

**What is tested.**
- (2) One boundary, natively: `committee_handoff_period_update_then_finality_signed_by_the_next_committee` (`attest/program/tests/instance.rs`). A store in period 1410 refuses the finality update signed in 1411, accepts the period-1410 update (next committee learned), then accepts that finality update and rotates.
- (1) Within one period on a chain: the rc1 `attest1` instance, left at its checkpoint (slot 11564960), was advanced later by the proved update (§9.0).
- **Not tested:** two or more boundaries; a rotation by a period update rather than a finality update (the fixtures `update_period_1410.ssz` and `update_period_1411.ssz` would allow a two-step test); any proved period update on any chain.

**The CLI picks the wrong update for a long stall.** When the beacon node's finality update is not usable, `fetch_update` (`lez-bridge/cli/src/attest.rs`) asks for the period update of the store's own period. That is right while the store does not know its next committee. Once it does, the store needs period P+1's update, and re-asking for P's gives one that does not advance it. An operator can submit the right file by hand (`attest update --update-file … --kind period`); that path is not tested either.

**M3 changes:** the updater asks for period P while the next committee is unknown and P+1 once it is known; a catch-up test over at least three consecutive Sepolia periods (fixtures fetched from a beacon API) that rotates through period updates; one deliberate multi-period stall and catch-up on a chain; the weak-subjectivity refusal, so an instance past the bound refuses updates and points consumers to its replacement (F10).

### 9.7 Ethereum forks

- **Supported forks:** genesis through Gloas. Helios at `2af9b418` handles headers and signing domains up to Gloas, and `InstanceConfig.forks` has eight write-once slots in that order; an unscheduled fork has epoch `u64::MAX` (`attest/core/src/lib.rs`).
- **Sepolia** runs Gloas (fork epoch 353,024 in `attest/fixtures/sepolia/meta.json`). **Mainnet** has no Gloas epoch yet (`GLOAS_FORK_EPOCH: 18446744073709551615` in `configs/mainnet.yaml`, consensus-specs `313cbfe`). So a mainnet instance created today stops verifying when mainnet activates Gloas: its updates are then signed under a fork version the instance does not know. The same effect, shown with Fulu: `a_mainnet_instance_with_fulu_scheduled_later_refuses_the_real_update_by_its_signature` (`attest/program/tests/audit.rs`).
- **Adopting a fork (documented in M3):** once the fork's epoch is published, anyone initialises a new instance with the full schedule before that epoch, and consumers move to it (F10). Claims under anchors the old instance recorded stay valid. A fork after Gloas (`HEZE` and later, unscheduled in `configs/mainnet.yaml`) has no slot in the config or in Helios's `Forks`: it needs a Helios update and a new program image.
- **For the bridge** this is heavy. A bridge config is bound to one instance (`InitAttested`; since the 2026-11-14 fixes also through the root-source id in `cfgId`), so each Ethereum fork means a new bridge deployment and vault version. The old vault should stop taking deposits before the fork epoch, after a last update has anchored its deposits; it keeps releasing against its own exit roots (§10). A config that can name a successor instance would avoid the new vault; we have not designed one.

### 9.8 The sync-committee attack, quantified (RFP-021 DR "Trust assumptions", Sup6, VS4)

**Facts** (consensus-specs at `313cbfe`, read with `gh api` on 2026-11-14):
- 512 seats per period (`SYNC_COMMITTEE_SIZE`, `presets/mainnet/altair.yaml`), one period every 256 epochs, about 27.3 h.
- Seats are drawn from the active validators with probability proportional to effective balance, duplicates allowed (`get_next_sync_committee_indices`, `specs/electra/beacon-chain.md`). Each period's committee is in the beacon state one period ahead (`next_sync_committee`), so the targets are known about 27 h in advance.
- **No slashing.** Block bodies carry only proposer and attester slashings (`BeaconBlockBody`, `specs/electra/beacon-chain.md`; Gloas's body likewise). Signing a false header for light clients costs the signers no stake.
- Our threshold: instances require at least 342 signers (`MIN_SUPERMAJORITY`, `InstanceConfig::validate`; a policy of 341 is refused in `a_wrong_checkpoint_or_bootstrap_is_refused`), and Helios applies an update only with 3 × signers ≥ 2 × 512.

**What it costs**, two ways:
1. **By holding stake.** With a share f of all effective balance, an attacker's seats in a period are close to Binomial(512, f) (draws with replacement, weighted by balance). Computed by us with the exact binomial tail: P(≥ 342 seats) is about 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ at f = 1/2, 8.9 × 10⁻⁴ at f = 0.6, and 0.50 at f ≈ 2/3. With about 321 periods a year, half of all stake gives about 4 × 10⁻¹² a year. By stake alone the attack needs about two thirds of all staked ETH, which would already let the attacker finalize what it likes on Ethereum.
2. **By bribing or compromising seat holders.** It needs the holders of ≥ 342 of the 512 seats in one period. Their stake is not at risk, so stake is not the price. The price is what those operators would accept, plus what they lose if caught (reputation, clients, possible social action), and no public figure measures it. The often-quoted 342 × 32 ETH = 10,944 ETH counts the minimum activation balance (`MIN_ACTIVATION_BALANCE` 32 ETH, `presets/mainnet/electra.yaml`) of 342 distinct validators. It is neither the price nor a lower bound: seats repeat, and since Electra one validator can hold up to 2,048 ETH (`MAX_EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_ELECTRA`). Because seats follow stake, a few large operators holding about two thirds of stake between them are the realistic colluders. We give no ETH figure for this route, and we state that it is unslashable.
- **Once is enough.** A colluding committee can sign a false period update whose next committee is of its own choosing. From then on the attacker signs anything without further collusion. The only way out is a new instance from an independently checked checkpoint (F10).
- **A higher participation floor raises the bar** (proposal, §6.1a). At 427 signers (5/6), P(≥ 427 seats) is about 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁷ at f = 2/3 and 2.9 × 10⁻² at f = 0.8 (computed), and bribery needs 427 seats instead of 342.

**What it buys against this bridge** (read in the code):
- **On LEZ:** a false anchor lets anyone prove a fabricated vault root and mint wrapped tokens with no deposit behind them. There is no LEZ claim cap (deviation F11; an optional cap is M4), so the mint freeze, global or per token, is the only stop, and honest holders are diluted until it acts.
- **On Ethereum, once G2 exists: the unclaimed deposits are at risk.** On the RFP-021 path no deposit root is published, so G2's `depositRootsUsed` is empty and the vault's `UnknownDepositRoot` check does not apply (`outbound/core/src/journal.rs`, "On the RFP-021 path nothing goes there"). What holds is the vault's own count: per (token, denomination) claims may not exceed deposits (`ClaimsExceedDeposits`), and burns may not exceed claims (`BridgeVault.sol` `update`). So forged claims, burned and released, can take up to the deposited-but-unclaimed value of each (token, denomination), within the release caps per window and after the release delay, unless the freezer acts first. Honest depositors who then claim push claims above deposits, and every later `update` reverts, halting outbound (`research/failsafe-design.md` §1, row (a)). Holders who already claimed keep their backing (their claims were within `claims ≤ deposits`), but once outbound halts they cannot exit either; they can exit only until the halt. **Today, and with no-go, nothing is released at all**, so the loss is confined to LEZ.
- **For any other consumer of the attestation:** a false statement about Ethereum state, of whatever value that consumer attaches to it.

**What a consumer can do (VS4):**
- freeze, on LEZ (mint) and on Ethereum (releases), each half alone (built);
- the vault's release caps and release delay (built), which bound what forged claims can take per window once G2 exists;
- an optional LEZ claim cap per window (M4);
- a minimum anchor age for claims (proposal, §6.1a, M4);
- the higher participation floor above.

## 10. Recovery, configurations, upgrades, failsafe

- **Recovery (F9, R5), (1) local** (`lez-bridge/client/`, crate docs of `client/src/lib.rs`; `cli/src/recover.rs`; commit `0d67d3e`):
  - **Root per account,** from key material the LEZ wallet re-derives from its recovery phrase (a public account's signing key, a private account's authorization secret key): `PRK = HMAC-SHA256("RFP022/bridge/root/v1", account_secret)`.
  - **Per position:** `s = HMAC-SHA256(PRK, "RFP022/bridge/key/v1" ‖ cfgId ‖ kind ‖ accountId ‖ index ‖ ctx ‖ 0x01)`, where `ctx` binds every other input of the commitment. Deposits use `k = SHA256("RFP022/ck" ‖ s)`, exits `k' = SHA256("RFP022/ek" ‖ s')`. Binding every input means a reused index gives the same `C` (the vault refuses it before taking funds) or an unrelated secret.
  - **Scan:** the vault's `Deposit`, `StateUpdated`, `Released` and `RelayFeeMenuSet` logs (tree rebuilt and checked against every logged root) and every LEZ block naming the bridge config; per account and tuple, indexes until 20 unused in a row. Deposits come out claimable or claimed; exits burned, in the exit tree, or released.
  - **Run:** `scripts/e2e-recovery.sh` and `e2e-recovery-extra.sh` (run `recovery1`, 11-13) on anvil and the local LEZ chain with public accounts and a mock-verified update and release: `recover` from the wallet alone and after a restore from the phrase matched the script's truth table; after a shallow restore it matched with `--account-gap`. A private account's derived deposit was recovered and its claim **dry-run only**.
  - **Scope:** only seed-derived positions are recoverable. Deposits made with a raw `--secret`, and deposits made on the web page (random `s` in a claim file), are not found by the scan.
  - **What the scan reveals:** the Ethereum RPC sees one vault-wide log filter; the LEZ sequencer sees block downloads (`--lez-mode point` asks for each found nullifier account instead; opt-in).
  - **Not settled:** the Names Wallet provider SDK (built on a branch that is not published yet) derives app secrets with a different salt (`logos-names/app-secret/v1`), so the two give different secrets for the same account. The bridge has not adopted the SDK's derivation yet.
- **Configurations (F15, F16):**
  - `cfgId` pairs one vault with one bridge deployment, (1);
  - deployments are isolated (`bridge/tests/isolation.rs`, `vault/test/CrossConfig.t.sol`);
  - one bridge program deployment holds **one** config, so several entities = several deployments;
  - several configs under one program is not built.
  - LNS-name discovery is (3).
- **Upgrades (S8):**
  - pinned per vault version: verifier, `updateImageId`, `releaseImageId`, risc0 control root, LEZ's `PRIVACY_PRESERVING_CIRCUIT_ID`, L1 checkpoint and k;
  - a change means a new vault and bridge (as v1 → v2 on Sepolia);
  - the old vault stops deposits but releases forever against its own exit roots.
- **Failsafe (S9), (3):** `research/failsafe-design.md` (2026-11-13; all its byte and cycle figures are estimates).
  1. per-token or global wind-down: freeze both halves;
  2. G2 publishes a final claim accumulator (an indexed Merkle tree keyed by the claim-nullifier PDA);
  3. each depositor reclaims on Ethereum through a separate immutable reclaim module, proving their deposit and the non-membership of its nullifier (a new guest, G4), with a credential derived from `s` that no claim publishes;
  4. unreleased burns release normally.
  - It covers unclaimed deposits for a bug in inbound verification or in G3. For a bug in G2, in the proof system or in vault accounting, and for wrapped tokens on LEZ, it documents that no compliant mechanism exists on LEZ 0.4.
  - **Not implemented.** Of its staged plan, the accumulator crate (`outbound/claim-acc`) and the `s → k` convention (`client/`) exist as parts of other work. The wind-down, the reclaim module, G4 and their tests (W3–W9) are not built.
